So far I know and understand two causes of unhappiness.

The first cause is a gap between what we want and what we have, without an understanding of how to close it.

We consume information one way or another, and it breeds dissatisfactions. Our ideal picture of the world gains an object that is not yet real. If the thinking stops there, a person falls into mental frustration, and that is what brings discomfort and unhappiness.

A simple example: after watching a reality show on YouTube, someone wants to live large like the people in the videos. But he has no money. He can't fulfil the wish, and day after day it wears down his mental health.

There are two ways out of the first cause.

The first is to analyse the wish and drop it as unneeded. Our social environment plants wishes in us in the background, often without our noticing. Bringing such a wish to the surface through reflection, and asking whether it makes sense, can help. For the most part, productive people are not the ones who do many different things, but the ones who dropped all those things and put their effort into something that matters to them.

The second way: if the dissatisfaction is still important after the analysis, look for a way to resolve it. Most often this starts with admitting that we lack mastery. Otherwise we would already be halfway there. Realising the lack of mastery is an important step out of the abyss, because it sets everything else in motion. To identify the mastery we need, we need a broad outlook and a developed intellect — one more reason to keep developing ourselves throughout life. Then the cycle starts: mastering the skill, applying a practical method, correcting the strategy. That cycle is what brings a person out of frustration.

The second cause is having two wishes that contradict each other.

Life has plenty of examples. We want to do something selfish, but we also want other people to like us. To go on holiday to a faraway country, and to save up for a mortgage down payment. We don't feel like working, but we need money to live.

The first step is to find the two conflicting wishes and admit they exist. Once the contradiction comes out into the open, the discomfort already eases. The second step is to decide and choose just one. Or to tell yourself "I'll decide later" — that works too. It won't remove the discomfort completely, but it puts us into agent mode: we have found the dissatisfaction and can make hypotheses about how to resolve it.

Unhappiness and discomfort add up from many small unresolved problems, breaches through which mental energy leaks away. If there is stress but no clarity about where it comes from, make yourself stop-moments and think about the possible causes. After that, only work helps: learn, think, act.
