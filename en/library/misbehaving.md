## Brief takeaways
A few short and interesting thoughts that hooked me, I'll leave right here.

*Hindsight bias* - a common cognitive bias in management and finance - is the tendency to perceive events that have already happened as obvious and predictable. This perception distorts memory by reproducing past experience incorrectly, and leads to false theoretical conclusions. In financial analysis this error can lead to overconfidence in how accurately an expert can predict a future swing in a stock's price.

It's important to understand that people's time to think and their brainpower are always limited. That's why they often use heuristics to make judgements simpler. In similar situations and circumstances people can follow a common heuristic and make predictable errors. Classical models of economic decision-making assume from the start that the errors people make can swing either way, positive or negative (be random), so they will balance each other out and can be ignored. But human errors are not random, they can be predicted by the influence of so-called *supposedly irrelevant factors*, and in many cases people's use of heuristics can push the result in a predictable direction, unlike in the classical rational model.

Life is felt as change. As the relative change in life gets smaller, the same gains and losses are felt less vividly. Finding 10 bucks on the street as a schoolkid with no pocket money brings far more happiness than finding the same money as an engineer with a salary of 5000. And losing 10 dollars feels about twice as painful as gaining them.
